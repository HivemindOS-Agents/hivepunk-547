Uniswap v4 is the fourth major release of the Uniswap protocol, live since 31 January 2025, running all pools in a single PoolManager contract and letting developers attach hooks that customise fees, swaps and liquidity. It is worth knowing now because pages report protocol fees reached v4 pools in July 2026, aggregator hooks launched in October 2026, and v4 has settled over $484 billion in cumulative volume across 20 networks.

## Thesis

Uniswap v4 is programmable infrastructure rather than a fixed AMM: its singleton, flash accounting and immutable hook choice enable custom fees and routing, but that same hook flexibility creates per-pool pricing, MEV and security risks that pages do not quantify by hook.

## What it is

All pools live inside one singleton PoolManager rather than a new contract per pool, trades settle through flash accounting using transient storage, and a hook contract chosen at pool creation can run code before or after swaps, liquidity changes, initialisation and donations. The core logic is described as non-upgradeable, while each pool can use its own hook, and a pool's hook cannot be changed once set.

## Risks

- Custom hooks dramatically expand the potential attack surface, and a single misconfigured or malicious hook can lead to significant financial losses, denial-of-service or pool manipulation.
- Hook logic can create exploitable asymmetries versus plain v3 quotes, requiring per-pool simulation and breaking universal arb bots designed for v2/v3 invariants.
- Dynamic-fee hooks can be front-run or back-run around predictable oracle fee changes.
- Users signing v4 transactions face arbitrary hook code running during their swap, not just a curve and fee tier.
- Example label in searcher analysis shows per-trade arb sizes on hook-altered pools in early 2026 of $30-$2,000 with much higher variance than v3, indicating uneven pricing on untested curves.
